Direction 3 of 3 — SETLIST editorial / monumental · "quiet authority, type-led, archival confidence"

Rationale (3 sentences): Setlist treats every night as a printed edition of record — dated masthead, one hairline chronology, artist names set monumentally — because a system of record should look like one, and archival confidence is calm certainty made typographic. The serif spark lines sit inside the big condensed grotesque like pull-quotes in a great music page, so curiosity gaps open with editorial credibility rather than promotional heat. Restraint is the differentiation: in a category of glowing gradients, the product that looks like it will still be right in year three reads as the one that keeps its word.

Deliberately avoids: Resident Advisor's gray austerity (Setlist is bone-on-ink with a letterpress stamp, warm not clinical), DICE's poster minimalism (we carry structure — the time rule — not emptiness), corporate ecommerce chrome, and all badge/checkmark vocabulary.

Type pairing: Archivo Expanded/condensed grotesque display (fallback system condensed) + Georgia text serif. Signature element: THE TIME RULE + EDITION MASTHEAD — the night as a dated, printed setlist; gone tomorrow.
