# ONE LIVE — The Mantle v1: claiming thought leadership + execution default for AI-era basics in local culture

**Compiled 2026-07-22 · Status: PROPOSAL — research response to the founder's fifth 2026-07-22 directive: assess the opportunity to grab the thought-leadership AND execution mantle for small businesses — especially cultural businesses and people — and claim the position of the "default" and "no-brainer": if they want more, fine, there are folks who deliver more — but nobody should have to pay for the basics, the table stakes, the core (as defined in the E-standard) to engage with the fast-approaching new world. Companion to `ONE_LIVE_B2A_GEO_MARKET_ASSESSMENT_v1.md` (esp. §8 onliness, §14 beyond-core, §15 Q9–Q13) and `ONE_LIVE_OWNED_AGENT_v1.md` (E1–E7). Nothing here is license to build or publish.**

Greppable summary: the mantle is genuinely unclaimed — every incumbent is
structurally barred from saying "the basics should be free" (vendors sell
them, agencies bill them, platforms monetize them, Bandsintown is
music-only with no verification story). Category-design economics say the
claim is worth taking: category creators captured 51% of revenue growth
and 80% of market cap among fast growers; kings take 70–80% of category
economics. Precedents mapped: Let's Encrypt (execution mantle — made free
HTTPS the default for 700M+ sites) and HubSpot (thought-leadership mantle
— named "inbound," gave away the academy). Strategy: claim BOTH mantles in
sequence — narrative assets now (the Standard, the Manifesto, the naming),
execution proof at Phase A (the Mirror roadshow, the Report, the
scoreboard), the word "default" only when coverage metrics earn it. Po
battery run (seed 2026072202, word "telescope", harvest H-M1–M6). Cost:
~zero cash, real founder hours. Questions Q14–Q17.

## 2. Why the mantle is unclaimed (and why that won't last)

Every actor with the resources to claim this position is structurally
barred from the claim itself:

| Actor | Why they can't say "the basics should be free" |
|---|---|
| GEO/AEO tools (Profound et al.) | The basics ARE the product — $29–$50k/mo |
| Agencies | The basics are the retainer ($1.5k–$25k/mo) |
| Yext-class listings vendors | The basics are the subscription ($200–500/yr/loc) |
| Google/Meta/Yelp | The basics are the ad-funnel bait; "free" means "we monetize you" |
| Bandsintown | Closest — but music-only, artist-side, no verification story, and its business model is promotion services |
| Chambers/arts councils | Have the trust, lack the execution machinery |

**Nobody is speaking FOR small cultural businesses in the AI transition** —
they are being spoken AT (fear marketing: "you're invisible in AI, pay
us"). The vacuum is real, it is specific to the down-market/cultural
segment, and it closes when the first funded player goes freemium
down-market (assessment §5 rivalry: ~12–18 months).

**The economics of claiming it:** [category creators in the Fortune
fast-growers captured 51% of cumulative revenue growth and 80% of market
capitalization; category kings take 70–80% of a category's
economics](https://www.playbigger.com/media/category-design-a-voyage-toward-belief)
([overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_design)). We are not
sizing a revenue category (ours is free) — we are sizing DEFAULT STATUS:
the same dynamics decide who becomes the reflex answer to "what do I do
about AI?" for a venue owner.

## 3. The two mantles, and the precedent for each

**Execution mantle — Let's Encrypt.** Made basic HTTPS free and automatic;
[now serves 700M+ websites and ~64% of the certificate
market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Encrypt); web encryption went
[from 39% of page loads in 2015 to dominant](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/resources/case-studies/lets-encrypt).
The mantle move wasn't a manifesto first — it was REMOVING THE REASON TO
PAY, wrapped in credible stewardship (nonprofit, principles published,
automation so "free" also meant "easier"). Lesson: the execution mantle is
earned by the product being the path of least resistance, and the
stewardship FRAME (open, principled, not-a-trick) is what converts "free
tool" into "the default."

**Thought-leadership mantle — HubSpot.** Coined "inbound marketing," then
[gave away the education: HubSpot Academy courses, certifications, free
tools](https://www.coursera.org/articles/hubspot-certification) — the
category name became the industry's vocabulary and the academy became a
distribution engine. Lesson: NAME the thing, TEACH the thing free, and let
certification/curriculum carry your worldview into every conversation
where you're not in the room.

**OneLive's position is unusually strong for claiming BOTH** because the
gate gives the narrative something no marketer has: mechanical proof. "We
couldn't sell you a ranking if we wanted to — the physics forbid it" is a
thought-leadership claim that survives hostile audit, which is exactly
what makes a mantle durable.

## 4. The claim, framed (naming + manifesto direction — founder voice decisions)

**The position sentence (candidate, for founder edit):** *"Being findable,
accurate, and fairly represented in the AI era is table stakes — and table
stakes should be free. For every venue, artist, and organizer. Forever.
If you want more than the basics, hire help proudly — but never pay
anyone for the basics again."*

**The named artifact (candidate names, pick at ratification):** publish
E1–E7 as an open, versioned public standard — working title **"The
Presence Standard for Live Culture"** (alts: "The Table Stakes Standard",
"The Culture Presence Baseline"). Anyone may implement it — including our
competitors and consultants; we are its AUTHOR and STEWARD. Giving it away
is the point: the author of the checklist owns the conversation about the
checklist (HubSpot lesson), and "even our competitors use our definition
of the basics" IS the mantle.

**The manifesto:** one page, founder-signed, plain language: what the AI
wave means for a venue in one paragraph; the seven basics; the promise
(free forever, no rank for sale, you hold the string); the honest line
about "more than basics" (consultants have a real place — §14 of the
assessment names them REFER-class respectfully); the invitation. Tone
canon: never fear-marketing — the industry sells fear; we sell settledness
("this part is handled").

## 6. Sequencing law (the Black-hat rule this plan obeys)

**Never claim the execution mantle ahead of the execution.** The single
fastest way to lose a mantle is vaporware — claiming "default" status with
zero claimed entities hands the story to the first skeptic. Sequence:

1. **Now → Step 9 (narrative prep, nothing public):** naming decision,
   manifesto draft, Standard's plain-language rendering, glossary,
   partner-org shortlist. All drafts live in the repo; NOTHING publishes
   before the founder's go and a live /tonight.
2. **Step 9–10 / Phase A (narrative launch + execution proof together):**
   Standard + Manifesto publish WITH the working claim flow — the
   announcement IS a working product ("here's the standard, here's the
   free tool that meets it in five minutes"). First workshops. First
   sampled mini-Report if the Mirror is cleared (Q10), else the Report
   waits — the manifesto doesn't need it.
3. **Phase B+ (default status, earned):** the word "default" enters our
   copy only when coverage says so (assessment §4 SOM: 30–50% of active
   Austin venues claimed). Before that we are "the free standard," not
   "the default." The Report goes annual; curriculum scales through
   partners; scoreboard public.

## 7. Risks and costs, honestly

1. **Founder time is the real COGS.** Every asset above is cash-cheap and
   voice-expensive: the manifesto, press, workshops are founder-fronted at
   first. Mitigation: M-E's partner-delivered model moves the marginal
   hours to allies early.
2. **The mantle makes every accuracy slip newsworthy.** Claiming "the
   accuracy people" means one viral wrong listing costs more than it
   would cost an anonymous startup. This is acceptable ONLY because the
   Kaizen zero-escape discipline and the dispute mechanics already exist —
   the mantle is a bet on our own gates, which is the right bet or we
   shouldn't ship at all.
3. **We will be attacked from two sides:** agencies ("free = worthless,
   they're building a walled garden in disguise") and skeptics ("another
   AI company"). Counters are already built: open standard anyone can
   implement (not a garden), open feed (Q8), the §14 REFER list treating
   consultants respectfully (we're not anti-consultant, we're
   anti-charging-for-basics), artist-facing language that never says
   "AI-powered" (Part II §14 nuance).
4. **Overclaim risk on "no-brainer":** the phrase is a bar, not a slogan —
   if onboarding takes 20 minutes, the words become a liability. The
   one-field bar (H4) and E-standard acceptance criteria are the
   mechanical guard; marketing language stays behind product truth (§6).
5. **Trust-canon guard:** consumer surfaces keep the design canon (no
   badges, no "confirmed" text). Any partner-facing "meets the Standard"
   mark is a SEPARATE surface and a founder decision (Q17) — and it may
   never imply ranking, favoritism, or verification beyond what the gate
   actually did.
