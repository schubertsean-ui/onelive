## 0. The premise (founder-set, this session) and the positioning

**v1 ships, at launch:**
1. **All culture — every event of every kind, not just music.** A culture-and-
   entertainment product across all ~22 cultural domains, live on day one (not a
   music app with "earned" expansion).
2. **A dozen US markets**, simultaneously.
3. **"Text me events"** as a first-class surface.
4. **Anti-attention-economy positioning**, delivering **practical/tactical value
   now** — flourishing is the later chapter.
5. A **starter Heartbeat** (aggregate "what's happening / how much / where") at launch.
6. **Public-agency data** (special-event permits, health/fire, alcohol licenses,
   library APIs) as an in-scope source strategy.

**The positioning, in the founder's words:** *Open — against the grain of the
siloed, free-at-the-cost-of-privacy attention economy. Seek the least attention
(**Total Attention Cost**) to help people thrive by getting the information they
want, to do the things they want, to live the life they want.*

> **Note on the term.** "Total Attention Cost (TAC)" here is the same lens the
> navigation spec calls "Total Cost of Attention" — the whole price (time +
> cognitive + emotional) a person pays to answer "what should I do?" ONE LIVE wins
> by driving TAC toward zero while delivering the most real-world value per unit of
> attention. This document adopts the founder's phrasing.

## 1. The outside-in read of v1

An analyst looking at this v1 sees something categorically different from the music
apps and the ticket marketplaces:

- **Not a vertical, a *layer*.** Not "concerts near me" (Bandsintown), not "tickets"
  (Eventbrite/Fever), not "my friends' parties" (Partiful) — but *the whole of what's
  happening in my community, across everything, in one calm place.*
- **Not attention-maximizing, attention-*minimizing*.** Its explicit goal is to take
  as little of your attention as possible and give you your life back — the exact
  inverse of every feed. That is a brand position no incumbent can occupy.
- **Open, not walled.** It distributes truth outward (the source others read from)
  rather than trapping you inside.
- **The obvious question an analyst still asks:** *the need is real, but has anyone
  ever gotten people to actually switch to a dedicated "everything local" habit?*
  (History says no — §7. That is the bet, and it must be named, not assumed.)

## 2. The category no one owns: universal, neutral, all-culture local discovery

This is the heart of the v1 thesis, and the research backs it hard: **no player has
genuinely universal, neutral, all-culture coverage of a community — and the reason is
structural, not accidental.**

- **Walled gardens each cover only their monetizable slice.** Facebook Events
  (feed-buried, login-gated, organic reach collapsed ~16%→~1–2%), Eventbrite/Fever
  (only what runs on *their* ticketing rails; Fever even prioritizes events *it
  produces*), Bandsintown/Songkick (music only), Meetup (recurring groups only). Each
  covers what it can charge for.
- **The one open, broad player — Google "Things to do" — is limited by structured
  data.** It routes you *out* and ingests any `Event`-schema page, but the un-marked-up
  long tail is invisible and its ranking is opaque; it is not a curated cultural layer.
- **The most culturally valuable events are the least monetizable — so they're
  orphaned.** A poetry reading, a church fair, a gallery opening, a library talk, a
  park meetup, a neighborhood market: **no ticketing feed, no one willing to pay to
  list them.** Ad-driven and transaction-driven models are *economically indifferent*
  to exactly these events. (Research: local-music work finds "a large percentage of
  events in local newspapers are not listed on commercial services.")
- **The infrastructure that *used* to cover all-culture is dying.** Alt-weekly and
  daily-newspaper calendars were the truest community culture calendars; 3,200+ papers
  have vanished since 2005, taking those calendars with them. **The gap is widening,
  and nothing has filled it.**

**Why a walled garden structurally *cannot* be the universal neutral layer:** being
universal + neutral means covering the unmonetizable and refusing to rank by money —
which is incompatible with a business built on keeping you inside and monetizing your
attention or transactions. ONE LIVE can occupy this category *because* it monetizes
insight, tooling, and civic value rather than the hoarded listing. **All-culture +
neutral + open is the one position the incumbents can't take without breaking their
own model.** This is the strongest wedge in the whole strategy — far stronger than
"music discovery."

## 2A. The quality wedge: not just *complete* — *rich*. Great experiences.

Completeness is half the wedge. The other half is **depth per event** — the thing
that turns a listing into a great night — and it exploits the two ways *everything
that exists today is bad*:

- **Failure mode 1 — CLUTTER.** The ad/sponsorship players (Do512/DoStuff, Facebook
  feed) drown you in noise, sponsored junk, and pay-amplified filler. You can't find
  the good thing because money decided what's loud.
- **Failure mode 2 — THINNESS.** The calendars (Austin Chronicle, Bandsintown) give
  you a bare stub: *band + venue + time*. Nothing about **what kind of music it is,
  what the venue is like, or what's around it.** So you leave to go research it —
  more tabs, more time — or you just don't go.

**ONE LIVE's wedge is rich context *without* clutter** — the depth that lets someone
have a *great experience*, not just find a date on a calendar. Per event it answers:
- **What is it / what's it like?** the Spark Line + Emotion Glyph ("swamp-gospel
  stomp with brass and menace") — what kind of music/comedy/art, and what it will
  *feel* like.
- **What's the venue like?** venue truth — kind of place, room, vibe — strengthened
  by the public-agency layer (§4.2: health/ABC classification, fire-marshal capacity,
  "is this a listening room or a warehouse").
- **What's around it?** the before/after/nearby context (the whole-night lens) — so
  the night is planned, not just the one event.

**The elegant part: this depth *lowers* Total Attention Cost, it doesn't add to it.**
Incumbents force a choice — wade through clutter, or take a thin stub and go research
the band/venue/area yourself across five more tabs. ONE LIVE puts *exactly the context
you'd have gone hunting for* right at the point of decision, so you decide well in
seconds and go. **More useful context, less total work.** That is how "least attention"
and "great experiences" are the same move, not a tradeoff — and it is a **depth moat**
a cluttered or thin competitor cannot copy without becoming us.

## 3. The positioning weapon: anti-attention economy → practical value now

Your "against the grain of the attention economy" stance is not just branding; it is
**both the differentiation and the defense**, and it converts directly to practical
value a person feels immediately.

- **The practical promise (v1, not flourishing):** *reclaim the 20–45 minutes you'd
  spend hunting across a dozen tabs and group texts, and go have a **great
  experience** — not just find a date on a calendar, but know it's the right thing,
  what it'll be like, and what the night around it looks like (§2A).* Know what's on
  in your community — across everything — in seconds, and go live your life. The value
  is time and ease returned, and better nights, **today**.
- **Why it's a defense, not just a slogan:** the attention economy's whole machine
  (feeds, targeting, dwell-time optimization, "free at the cost of privacy") is
  something Meta/Google/TikTok *cannot* renounce — it's their revenue. ONE LIVE
  renouncing it is a *credible commitment they can't match.* "We take the least of
  your attention and never sell your data" is a promise only a company not built on
  attention can make.
- **The measurement inversion (from the nav spec):** success = **job-done-fast**
  (time-to-answer, taps-to-plan, decided-plan rate), never **time-on-app**. We publicly
  celebrate how *little* time people spend — the "app that wants you to leave." This is
  marketable, funny, and true.
- **Flourishing is the later chapter, on purpose.** v1 sells utility ("get the info
  you want, do what you want"); the deeper "help the culture thrive / community
  flourishing" story lands once the practical habit exists. Leading with flourishing
  before utility would be a pitch; leading with utility earns the right to the bigger
  story.

## 4. The mechanisms that make it real — and hard to copy

### 4.1 The all-culture pipeline (already generalized)
The fetch→extract→gate→promote pipeline is deliberately *not* music-specific — a
museum's calendar parses like a club's; the 22-domain taxonomy and per-domain
sub-categories already exist (`ONE_LIVE_CATEGORY_TAXONOMY_v1.md`). What v1 needs is
breadth of *sources* per domain per market, and the display discipline to keep it
calm: **one bounded river / daily edition, content classes as member-default lenses,
full city one tap away, no class ranked for money.** All-culture must feel like *your
community, filtered to you* — never a firehose.

### 4.2 Public-agency data — the long-tail unlock (research-confirmed)
This directly attacks the structural problem that killed everyone else ("the long tail
has no feed"). It's cheap, authoritative, ToS-clean (government facts aren't
copyrightable), and no competitor mines it:

- **City special-event permits = a government-verified *event* feed** (festivals,
  parades, street fairs, markets, races) — often *forward-looking* (NYC publishes
  approved events for the next ~30 days via open API; dataset `tvpp-9vvx`). These are
  the events happening *between* the venues that venue-scraping never sees.
- **Health-inspection data via the LIVES open standard + Socrata** = a continuously-
  refreshed, geocoded registry of *every real food/drink venue and what type it is*
  — a venue-truth backbone (closed places fall off the inspection cycle).
- **State alcohol licenses (TABC etc.)** = instantly classifies bar/brewery/winery/
  restaurant and confirms a licensed operating premises.
- **Library `LibCal` APIs** = a dense, standardized stream of *free* cultural events
  (author talks, workshops, kids' programming) across most US libraries.
- **Fire-marshal occupant load** = the only authoritative venue *capacity* number in
  existence (premium enrichment; usually per-address records, so selective).

**Access reality (honest):** the best sources are open-data APIs (Socrata `SODA` — one
adapter covers many cities); the rest need per-city mapping or public-records requests.
There is **no national standard for event/permit data** (LIVES exists only for food
inspections), so expect per-city plumbing — which is itself a moat (the work compounds;
a fast-follower starts at zero). **Strong-open-data cities** (NYC, Chicago, SF, Seattle,
LA, Austin/TX, Boston, DC, Denver, King County) should weight the dozen-market pick.

### 4.3 Text-me-events — the flagship low-TAC channel (viable, with real constraints)
SMS/RCS is arguably *the* lowest-Total-Attention-Cost channel: value pushed to you, a
finite 2–3 lines, **no app, no login, ~universal reach (incl. non-smartphones), ~98%
open rate.** The Community.com precedent (opt-in two-way SMS, 98% open in 5 min, ~30%
reply) proves the intimacy of the inbox sustains engagement feeds can't. It is
structurally anti-doomscroll — nothing to scroll.

**But name the constraints (don't sell it as free):**
- **Quiet-hours law fights an evening product.** Carrier/CTIA rules bar sends before
  8am / after 9pm recipient-local — so "what's on *tonight*" can't blast at 8pm. Design
  a **morning/afternoon "tonight" digest**, per timezone.
- **Linear cost, no ad offset.** ~$0.011–0.013 all-in per SMS; at 100k users × a few
  texts/week it's a real bill with no pay-to-rank to subsidize it. Mitigate with **RCS
  (verified branded sender, richer, falls back to SMS) + conservative default cadence +
  a personalized digest, not per-event blasts.**
- **Hard prerequisites:** TCPA written consent + A2P 10DLC brand/campaign registration
  (1–4 week approval — plan ahead), STOP/HELP, honor revocation. **"Never sell the
  number"** is *both* a carrier requirement and perfectly on-brand.
- **Frequency fatigue is the #1 killer** (every prior SMS product's failure mode);
  cadence must default conservative and be user-controlled. Message *personality/voice*
  drives love (Poncho, Community.com) — and the Anticipatory Greeting ("hey joe, more
  wine events or something else?") is exactly this channel's native voice.

### 4.4 Starter Heartbeat at launch
Ship the aggregate subset day one — cultural density/momentum per area/domain, on
resolved + licensed + public-agency data — as the "pulse of your community" surface and
the seed of the later analytics business. Aggregate-only, consent-gated, never
individual (the invariants are the product). Deep predictive/emotion analytics stay later.

## 6. The graveyard — and how ONE LIVE answers each killer

Local-events discovery is a documented startup graveyard (Upcoming.org, Songkick,
Zvents, Eventful all died; DoStuff survived only by narrowing to paid promotion). The
recurring killers map almost one-to-one onto ONE LIVE's design choices — which is the
strongest evidence the design is right:

| What killed them | ONE LIVE's answer (already canon) |
|---|---|
| **Monetized via ticketing → incumbents squeezed them** (Songkick, Zvents) | Not ticketing-dependent; monetize insight/tooling/civic — no ticket rail for Live Nation to cut off. |
| **Sold to a big platform that neglected it** (Upcoming→Yahoo, Eventful→CBS) | Neutrality entrenched as a dissolution trigger; the acquisition "observer effect" (companion doc §10) argues *against* a kill-and-absorb sale. |
| **Pay-to-rank gravity narrowed coverage** (DoStuff) | No-pay-to-rank is *physics* (CI-enforced), not a policy that erodes under revenue pressure. |
| **The long tail has no feed → incomplete** | Crawl/AI pipeline **+ public-agency data** (§4.2) captures the orphaned long tail cheaply. |
| **Per-market human curation cost didn't scale** | AI extraction + reusable per-source recipes + one Socrata adapter across many cities → the per-market cost curve bends down, not up. |

**The one killer that remains live: demand (§7).** Everything else, ONE LIVE's design
already answers.

## 7. The honest central risk: latent demand, and dozen-market execution

Two risks the analysis must confront rather than assume away — **without pretending the
core need is in doubt. It isn't.** This is a startup building something that does not
exist today, and the evidence it's needed is not abstract — *it's how bad the existing
options are.*

**(a) The bet is category-creation and habit-formation — not "does anyone want this."**
The demand evidence is concrete: **what's out there today stinks.** Do512 is cluttered
with sponsored junk (its own model amplifies who pays); the Austin Chronicle calendar
and Bandsintown give you a bare stub (band + venue + time) with none of what makes a
night worth going to (§2A). People *are* going out (Partiful ~400% YoY, Luma 5×) and the
*need* is rising (third-places/loneliness crisis; local-news collapse took the community
calendars with it) — they're just poorly served. So the risk is **not** "will people want
a complete, rich, junk-free picture of their community" — of course they will; nothing
delivers it. The honest risk is narrower and normal for a category creator: **converting
a real, poorly-served need into a switching habit**, since today people default to
word-of-mouth and Instagram. De-risk it the way a startup does:
- Lead with **practical utility and great experiences** (find the good thing, know
  what it's like, plan the night, go) — not abstraction;
- Make **text-me** the wedge that meets people *in a channel they already live in* — no
  app to adopt, no habit to switch, the lowest-friction on-ramp to the habit;
- Be **so much better than the junk/thin status quo** that the switch is obvious the
  first time — depth + completeness + no clutter is a felt difference, not a marketing
  claim;
- Instrument **job-done + return** honestly from day one, and read the signal straight.

**(b) A dozen markets at once multiplies execution and coverage risk.** Cold-start is
per-market; all-culture coverage quality must hold across every domain in every metro, or
the "complete picture" promise breaks on night one (a user finds the show you missed and
leaves). De-risk by **weighting market selection toward strong open-data cities** (§4.2),
front-loading public-agency + licensed feeds for baseline completeness, and treating
coverage-completeness as the launch gate, not an afterthought.

## 9. Premortem — "It's 2028. All-culture ONE LIVE lost. What killed it?"

Ranked; each with the pre-committed defense.

1. **The switch to a habit didn't happen** — people kept defaulting to
   Instagram/word-of-mouth despite the junk. (The *need* was never the question; the
   habit was.) *Defense:* be unmistakably better than the thin/cluttered status quo on
   night one (§2A); lead with utility + great experiences + text-me (meet them where
   they are); measure return honestly; don't mistake press for retention.
2. **Coverage broke the "complete picture" promise** in one or more of a dozen markets /
   some domains. *Defense:* public-agency + licensed baseline; completeness as the launch
   gate; weight open-data markets.
3. **Distribution starvation** — an incumbent made "things to do near you" one tap away.
   *Defense:* own text-me + a direct habit + be the cited source-of-record; neutrality
   they can't match.
4. **Dozen-market execution outran the team / the money.** *Defense:* AI + public-data
   bend the per-market cost curve; stage market launches against coverage readiness;
   instrument cost-per-verified-event.
5. **Text-me economics/fatigue** — cost outran value or frequency churned users.
   *Defense:* RCS+fallback, conservative cadence, personalized digest, user frequency
   control; the digest respects quiet hours.
6. **Fast-follower land grab.** *Defense:* compounding public-data + neutrality-as-physics;
   own the category narrative first.
7. **Pay-to-rank gravity** as revenue pressure mounts. *Defense:* it's a dissolution
   trigger, CI-enforced — physics, not willpower.

**Through-line:** the surviving risk is **habit-formation + coverage + execution across
a dozen markets** — not whether the need is real (it is — what exists today is junk or
too thin) or whether incumbents can copy it (they structurally can't).

## 10. Recommendations — what to do now

**Tier 1 (launch-critical)**
1. **Front-load public-agency + licensed feeds** for baseline all-culture completeness
   in each market — the cheapest path to the "complete picture" promise. Build the
   Socrata/LibCal/permit adapters first.
2. **Weight the dozen-market pick toward strong open-data cities** (NYC, Chicago, SF,
   Seattle, LA, Austin, Boston, DC, Denver…) so completeness is achievable.
3. **Ship text-me as the flagship low-TAC surface** — but with the compliance/cost/
   cadence discipline (§4.3): morning/afternoon "tonight" digest, RCS+SMS, 10DLC done
   before launch, conservative default cadence, "never sell your number."
4. **Instrument job-done + return + cost-per-verified-event** from day one; publicly
   frame success as *low* Total Attention Cost (the "app that wants you to leave").
5. **Ship starter Heartbeat** (aggregate community pulse) to seed the data clock and the
   later analytics business.

**Tier 2 (protect the position)**
6. **Entrench neutrality** (charter/PBC) — makes the anti-attention promise credible and
   un-absorbable (founder-crucial).
7. **Trademarks + trade-secret hygiene** (companion doc §6) — cheap, real.
8. **Generalize the supply relationship to all creators/organizers** (the entity agent,
   companion doc §9) — museums, theaters, comedians, community orgs maintain their truth
   once, appear everywhere.

**Tier 3 (as demand proves)**
9. Validate a **paid civic/enterprise Heartbeat pilot**; deepen analytics.
10. Add markets against coverage readiness, not calendar ambition.
