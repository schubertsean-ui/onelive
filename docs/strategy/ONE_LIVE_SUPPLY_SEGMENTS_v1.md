# ONE LIVE — Supply-Side Segments v1 (the 23 categories × 5 size tiers)

**Compiled 2026-08-01 · Status: RATIFIED 2026-08-01 — founder: "let's add to the canon the 23 segments and add this content" (decision record: `docs/memory/decisions/2026-08-01_supply-segments-canon.md`). This doc is the canonical segmentation vocabulary for supply-side strategy, outreach, and (when built, through normal gates) product taxonomy. The companion research content is research-grade, not canon — see status note at the end.**

Greppable summary: 23 supply-side categories in three groups (11 Venues &
Places, 6 Organizers & Groups, 6 Artists & People) crossed with 5 size
tiers (SOLO nonemployer · SMALL 1–9 · MEDIUM 10–49 · LARGE 50–249 ·
JUMBO 250+ — OECD bands plus the Census-grounded nonemployer split:
30.4M US nonemployers = 78.4% of establishments). Bars split from
breweries/wineries/distilleries at founder direction; the producers
category carries a three-sided revenue model (visit · product ·
experience/education). The persona ladder (P1 owner-does-everything →
P5 in-house+agencies) is the cross-cutting behavioral lens.

## 1. The five size tiers (canon)

| Tier | Definition | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| SOLO | Nonemployer (no staff) — most working artists, DJs, solo organizers | Census: 30.4M US nonemployers (78.4% of establishments), growing 2.7%/yr |
| SMALL | Micro-employer, 1–9 people | OECD micro band |
| MEDIUM | 10–49 people | OECD small band |
| LARGE | 50–249 people | OECD medium band |
| JUMBO | 250+ people | OECD large band |

Behavioral thresholds the bands approximate: the first hire · the first
designated marketer · the first marketing TEAM · the first
agency-of-record.

## 2. The 23 categories (canon)

**Venues & Places (11)**
1. Live music venues & clubs (independent)
2. Bars, pubs & cocktail lounges with programming
3. Breweries, wineries, distilleries & tasting rooms — three-sided model: the visit (taproom/tasting room), the product (bottles/retail/DTC club), the experience (ticketed tastings, tours, pairing dinners, classes & education)
4. Restaurants & cafés with events
5. Theaters & performing arts centers
6. Comedy clubs & rooms
7. Nightlife: dance clubs & DJ rooms
8. Galleries & independent art spaces
9. Museums & cultural institutions
10. Independent cinemas & screening rooms
11. Nontraditional & multi-use spaces (halls, churches, warehouses, record stores, bookstores)

**Organizers & Groups (6)**
12. Festivals (music, arts, food, neighborhood)
13. Independent promoters & presenters
14. Community orgs, nonprofits & arts councils
15. Recurring-scene organizers (open mics, jams, poetry slams, song circles)
16. Social-dance & movement communities
17. Markets, fairs & pop-up organizers

**Artists & People (6)**
18. Bands & musical acts
19. Solo musicians & singer-songwriters
20. DJs & electronic artists
21. Comedians & spoken-word performers
22. Theater & dance companies, performance troupes
23. Visual artists, makers & craft creators

## 3. Conventions

- An entity may carry multiple categories (a brewery hosting a festival;
  an artist who promotes a series); the PRIMARY category is the one whose
  economics the entity lives on. Watcher records carry category as data;
  category changes follow normal review like any candidate data.
- Categories are OPEN for future amendment by founder decision only
  (this list changed 22→23 by exactly that path); briefs and research
  update freely with evidence.
- Size tier is orthogonal to category; not all tiers exist in all
  categories (open-mic organizers are SOLO; museums start at MEDIUM).
