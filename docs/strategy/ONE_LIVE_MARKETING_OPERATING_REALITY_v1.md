**Compiled 2026-08-01 · Status: RESEARCH (sourced; expert estimates labeled) — companion to the RATIFIED segmentation canon `ONE_LIVE_SUPPLY_SEGMENTS_v1.md`; added to the repo at founder direction 2026-08-01. Greppable summary: five size tiers (SOLO/SMALL/MEDIUM/LARGE/JUMBO — canon
doc) analyzed for stack, personas, weekly flow, time and cash, tactic mix,
and the leak. Load-bearing findings: (1) below $10M revenue the binding
constraint is LABOR AND ATTENTION, not intent — a small operator already
"spends" $1.1k–$3.2k/month in own-attention value against $50–$500 cash;
(2) the one stack layer every tier maintains (organic social) is the layer
AI answers use least, while the orphaned layers (listings, measurement,
structured presence) are what answer engines read; (3) spend is INVERSELY
correlated with measured return at the small end — cash concentrates in
~$5:1 paid social while $36–42:1 email and ~$13:1 local SEO sit unworked,
because effort, not evidence, allocates the budget; (4) every commercial
fix is priced above the tier that needs it, so below the in-house line
integration is done by nobody; (5) the persona ladder is a churn map —
the only stable states are "owner does it forever" and "bought outright."

## 1. The tiers and one-line diagnoses

| Tier | Live-culture examples | Operating model | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| SOLO (nonemployer) | Working artist, DJ, solo organizer | The person IS the brand and the staff | Every marketing hour is a performance hour lost; ~$0 cash |
| SMALL (1–9) | 80–150-cap room; founder-run gallery | Owner at night, on a phone | High intent, 5–15 hrs/wk exhausted attention; below-social layers unmaintained |
| MEDIUM (10–49) | 2–4 venue group; 400-cap club; working festival | Insider/relative + freelancer or single-aspect shop | Someone owns Instagram; nobody owns the SYSTEM |
| LARGE (50–249) | Regional group; mid-size promoter; season theater | 1–3 in-house + small/specialty agencies | Competence arrives with coordination costs — three vendors, one brand |
| JUMBO (250+) | National chains, major promoters, flagship festivals | Department + AOR + specialty agencies | Coverage complete; the fight is efficiency (budgets flat at 7.7% of revenue; agency rosters being cut) |

## 2. The two currencies (fig_spend)

Monthly cost by tier, cash vs the value of owner/GM attention (est. at
$50–75/hr): SMALL runs $50–500 cash against $1.1k–$3.2k of attention —
the true budget is hidden in nights and weekends, 3–20× the cash. The
ratio inverts a thousandfold by JUMBO. **Products aimed at small culture
compete with time, not budgets.** Benchmarks: Gartner 2025 CMO survey —
budgets flat at 7.7% of revenue; Deloitte/Duke — 9.4% avg, B2C services
~10%; SBA guidance 7–8% under $5M; typical SMB digital outlay
$500–$2,500/mo; freelancers $300–$1,500/mo; agencies $500–$2k (small) /
$2k–$7.5k (mid) / $7.5k–$25k+ (enterprise).

## 3. Who carries the work (fig_labor) — the persona ladder

P1 owner-does-everything (only 20% of owners felt prepared for
creative/brand demands; 56% pulled off core ops weekly) → P2 the
insider/relative (cheap, trusted, leaves) → P3 freelancer ($300–$1.5k/mo;
scope stops at posting) → P4 single-aspect shop ($500–$3k/mo per aspect;
the specialty IS the boundary — the client becomes the integrator) → P5
in-house + agencies (full coverage; 39% of CMOs cutting agency budgets).
Only stable states: P1 forever, or P5 bought outright — everything
between churns.

## 4. Stack coverage (fig_stack) and tactic spend (fig_tactics)

Eleven stack layers × tiers: organic social is the only universally
maintained layer; listings/maps, SEO, email, measurement, and AI
visibility run none-to-patchy below LARGE. Tactic cash (est., excl.
in-house salaries): SOLO concentrates in boosts+tools; SMALL leads paid
social 35%; MEDIUM adds paid search ~20%; LARGE/JUMBO spread into media,
production, agency fees (Gartner: paid media 30.6% of budget; digital
61.1% of channel spend, 69% of digital to paid). Adoption vs return
(SMB): unpaid social 66% used; social ads 56% used at ~$5:1; paid search
~40% at ~$8:1 (avg SMB Google spend ~$1,840/mo); email 53% at $36–42:1;
SEO 53% at ~$13:1. **The money follows the easy layer; the returns live
in the orphaned layers.** Whoever removes the execution cost of the
high-ROI orphans re-allocates the segment's outcome without new spend.

## 5. The weekly flow and break points

SMALL: book act → midnight story → day-of post → doors; breaks when one
busy week stops the loop (the system is one person's spare attention).
MEDIUM: insider drafts Sunday → owner approves by text → freelancer
schedules; breaks when the insider leaves or vendors' seams go unowned.
LARGE: calendar → agency produces → team reviews → monthly report; breaks
at vendor seams. JUMBO: quarters-ahead planning; breaks at efficiency
pressure. Hours/week: 5–15 (SOLO/SMALL, the owner's nights; 43% of
owners ~6 hrs/wk on social alone; 56% have ≤1 hr/DAY for all marketing)
→ 15–25 (MEDIUM) → 80–160 (LARGE) → 500+ (JUMBO).

## 6. The leak (opportunity and lost revenue)

$10.3B/yr US sales lost to wrong/missing listings (Chatmeter) · 68% of
consumers would stop using a business over incorrect listing info
(Entrepreneur) · 83% of restaurant locations invisible in AI answers
(Uberall) · ~40% of concert tickets unsold because fans don't know
(Billboard/Bandsintown) · complete listings get ~70% more clicks · 76%
of local searchers visit within 24 hours (Data Axle). Expert-estimate
translation (labeled): a persistent discovery gap costing a 150-cap room
even 3–5 covers a night is $15k–$50k/yr — more than its entire cash
marketing budget — and no invoice ever arrives for it.
