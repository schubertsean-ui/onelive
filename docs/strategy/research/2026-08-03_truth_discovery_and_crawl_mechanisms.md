# Ingestion Engine Benchmark: Transferable Mechanisms from General-Purpose Search/Data Systems

Research complete across all five families. Below is the full report: named state-of-the-art mechanisms with citations, then a concrete transferable design for a county-scoped-but-globally-scalable events engine, ending with the ranked top-10.

## Family 1 — Google-class crawl/index architecture

### (a) Named mechanisms

1. **Mercator two-tier URL frontier (priority front queues + politeness back queues).** The canonical design: a prioritizer assigns each URL to one of K FIFO "front queues" by priority; a biased round-robin selector feeds "back queues," each pinned to a single host, with a min-heap of next-allowed-fetch times enforcing per-host politeness. ([Mercator / SRC Report 173](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs685/2002fa/mercator.pdf), [Stanford IR book ch. 20](https://nlp.stanford.edu/IR-book/pdf/20crawl.pdf), [Crawl frontier overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawl_frontier))
2. **Poisson change models + change-frequency estimators (Cho & Garcia-Molina).** Page changes are modeled as a Poisson process with rate λ; they verified this against 270 sites and derived refresh policies where, counterintuitively, *uniform* allocation beats naive proportional-to-change-rate allocation, and the optimal policy deliberately under-crawls pages that change faster than your budget can track. ([Effective Page Refresh Policies, ACM TODS 2003](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/958942.958945)). Their companion paper gives the estimator that matters when you can only observe "changed since last visit" (not how many times): with n visits and X observed changes, **λ̂ = −log((n − X + 0.5)/(n + 0.5))** changes per visit interval — bias-corrected and usable from tiny samples. ([Estimating Frequency of Change, ACM TOIT 2003](http://oak.cs.ucla.edu/cho/papers/cho-freq.pdf), [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/857166.857170)) A refinement worth knowing: Olston & Pandey's **information longevity** — schedule by the lifetime of the *content fragments* you care about, not raw page-change rate, since much page churn is ads/boilerplate. ([Recrawl Scheduling Based on Information Longevity](https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/vip/teach/IRcourse/3_crawling_snippets/other_notes/paper_crawl_longevity.pdf))
3. **Optimal freshness crawl under budget + politeness constraints (Kolobov et al., Microsoft/Bing).** Modern formulation: choose per-URL crawl rates maximizing importance-weighted freshness subject to a global request budget and per-host rate caps; solved with Lagrangian/convex methods and, when change rates must be learned online, reinforcement learning. Validated on 18.5M URLs crawled daily for 14 weeks. ([SIGIR 2019](http://erichorvitz.com/SIGIR_2019_crawl_under_politeness_constraints.pdf), [NeurIPS 2019 RL scheduling](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Staying-up-to-Date-with-Online-Content-Changes-for-Kolobov-Peres/597538b6d66fc1a79e657c3ee31527980387f24f), [code+data](https://github.com/microsoft/Optimal-Freshness-Crawl-Scheduling)). Recent extension: exploiting cheap-but-noisy change-indicating signals (sitemap pings, ETag/Last-Modified) inside the same framework. ([arXiv 2502.02430](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02430), [Change Rate Estimation and Optimal Freshness](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.02167))
4. **Structured-data and push shortcuts around crawling.** Google's Event rich results consume **schema.org/Event JSON-LD** directly — name/startDate/location parsed with zero learned extraction ([Google Event structured data docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/event?hl=en)); sitemap `lastmod` is used to prioritize recrawl and *penalized when it lies* ([sitemaps & indexing guide](https://www.growthstats.io/blog/sitemaps-and-indexing-technical-seo-guide)); **IndexNow** inverts pull to push — publishers ping on change ([indexnow.org FAQ](https://www.indexnow.org/faq)), and **WebSub** does the same for feeds ([WebSub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebSub)).

### (b) Transferable design for 1Live

- **Frontier**: a `source_url` table is your frontier, not an in-memory queue. Columns: `url, source_id, host, tier (priority 0-3), lambda_hat, n_visits, x_changes, last_fetch_at, next_fetch_at, content_hash, fragment_hash, etag, last_modified_hdr`. The scheduler is a cron that selects `next_fetch_at <= now()` ordered by tier, then groups by host with a per-host token bucket (politeness). This is Mercator's two-tier design collapsed into SQL — right-sized for thousands of sources per county, and it shards by host trivially at national scale.
- **Change tracking**: on every fetch, compute two hashes — whole-page and *event-fragment* hash (hash of the extracted candidate set, which is Olston/Pandey's longevity insight: a venue page whose ads rotate daily but whose calendar changes weekly has fragment-λ ≪ page-λ). Update `x_changes` only on fragment change; recompute `lambda_hat = -ln((n - x + 0.5)/(n + 0.5)) / mean_interval`.
- **Recrawl rule**: `next_fetch_at = last_fetch + clamp(k / (lambda_hat × importance), min_interval, max_interval)`, where **importance for an events engine is time-to-event**: any URL advertising an event <72h away gets a T-minus schedule (e.g., recheck at T-48h, T-24h, T-6h) regardless of λ — cancellations and time changes cluster near the event. This importance-weighting under a budget is exactly Kolobov's formulation, and the budget cap maps onto the charter's cost ceilings.
- **Cheap-signal preflight**: before a full fetch+extract, do a conditional GET (ETag/If-Modified-Since) and check sitemap `lastmod` / RSS/iCal feeds; only spend extraction (LLM) tokens on changed fragments. This is the noisy-signal crawl optimization ([arXiv 2502.02430](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02430)) and directly serves cost discipline.
- **JSON-LD first**: extraction is a waterfall — (1) schema.org/Event JSON-LD or microdata if present (deterministic parse, near-zero cost, highest a-priori extraction confidence), (2) iCal/RSS feeds, (3) learned per-source template (Family 5), (4) LLM extraction as the *last* resort. Google runs exactly this shortcut; most venue-platform sites (Squarespace, Wix, Eventbrite embeds, Bandsintown widgets) emit Event JSON-LD.

## Family 2 — Truth discovery / data fusion from conflicting sources

### (a) Named mechanisms

1. **TruthFinder (Yin & Han, KDD 2007)** — the founding iterative algorithm: source trustworthiness and fact confidence estimated *from each other* until fixpoint. Trust of source w = mean confidence of its claims; confidence of fact f from independent sources: σ(f) = 1 − Π_w(1 − t(w)), computed in log space via τ(w) = −ln(1 − t(w)) so evidence adds: σ*(f) = Σ_w τ(w); partial-agreement between similar values propagates ("implication"), and a dampening factor γ compensates for non-independent sources. ([KDD paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1281192.1281309), [reference implementation](https://github.com/IshitaTakeshi/TruthFinder)). Successors — Latent Credibility Analysis (models "knowing" vs "asserting" truth), and the comparative evaluations — are surveyed in [Truth Discovery Algorithms: An Experimental Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.6428) and [Li et al.'s survey](https://research.usq.edu.au/download/0139ae81792a5393de4828ffbb80aa8d97319c6b921e9f06249b3004f9fc8d000/2291991/A_Survey_on_Truth_Discovery_Concepts_Methods_Applications_and_Opportunities.pdf).
2. **Knowledge Vault (Dong et al., KDD 2014)** — web-scale probabilistic fusion: every fact carries a **calibrated probability** combining (extractor reliability × source reliability × prior from existing KB); 1.6B triples, of which only the ≥90%-probability slice (271M) is treated as "confident." The key architectural idea: *extraction confidence and source truthfulness are separate estimated quantities*. ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Knowledge-vault:-a-web-scale-approach-to-knowledge-Dong-Gabrilovich/cf5ea582bccc7cb21a2ebeb7a0987f79652bde8d), [From Data Fusion to Knowledge Fusion](https://lunadong.com/talks/fromDFtoKF_vldb.pdf))
3. **Knowledge-Based Trust (Dong et al., VLDB 2015)** — estimate a *source's* trustworthiness endogenously, from the correctness of the facts it asserts (against the fused consensus), with a multi-layer probabilistic model that distinguishes "the extractor misread the page" from "the page is wrong." ([VLDB paper PDF](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p938-dong.pdf), [Dong's publication list](https://lunadong.com/publications))
4. **Fellegi–Sunter probabilistic record linkage** (also the backbone of Family 3): per-field m-probability (agreement given match) and u-probability (agreement given non-match); **agreement weight = log₂(m/u), disagreement weight = log₂((1−m)/(1−u))**; posterior match odds = prior odds × Π 2^w_i; parameters trained *without labels* via EM (Winkler/Jaro). ([Splink's Fellegi-Sunter topic guide](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html), [EM intuition — Robin Linacre](https://www.robinlinacre.com/em_intuition/))

### (b) Transferable design for 1Live

This family is 1Live's core moat. Concrete design:

- **Claims table, not facts table**: `claim(candidate_event_id, attribute, value, source_id, extractor_id, extraction_conf, observed_at)`. Never overwrite; the gate fuses claims per attribute. This is Knowledge Vault's separation: `P(fact) = f(extraction_conf, source_reliability, prior)`.
- **Source reliability as an estimated, evolving quantity** — the KBT/TruthFinder loop, run nightly:
  1. For each attribute-value, fused confidence `σ*(v) = Σ_{sources asserting v} −ln(1 − t(s) · extraction_conf)`, then σ = 1 − e^(−γ·σ*) with γ ≈ 0.3 dampening correlated sources (two aggregators syndicating the same feed are NOT independent — keep a `syndication_parent` edge and count a syndicate family once).
  2. `t(s)` ← smoothed mean of σ over claims s made **that were later resolvable** (event happened/was corroborated/was disputed). Use Beta-Bernoulli update so new sources start at a weak prior: `t(s) = (α + confirmed) / (α + β + resolved)` with e.g. α=1, β=1 — a formula the trust_gate can own and test.
  3. Iterate 1–2 to fixpoint (converges in <10 iterations in practice per the [experimental evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.6428)).
- **Gate rule as odds, not counts**: replace "N sources agree" with "fused log-odds ≥ threshold per confidence state." `unverified` = single low-trust claim; `likely` = fused σ ≥ 0.7; `confirmed` = fused σ ≥ 0.95 **and** ≥1 claim from a source-class flagged authoritative (venue's own site/ticketing); `disputed` = two value-clusters each above σ 0.5 for the same attribute (time conflict, cancellation conflict). This makes "disputed shown-never-hidden" a *mechanical* output of fusion, and it's exactly Knowledge Vault's calibrated-probability slice mapped onto the ratified 4-state model.
- **Value implication**: TruthFinder's partial-credit trick matters for events — "8:00 PM doors" and "8:30 PM show" partially corroborate rather than conflict; encode an attribute-specific similarity kernel (time within 60 min = 0.5 implication; same-day = weak; different day = negative implication → dispute signal).

## Family 3 — Fuzzy matching & incomplete-data systems

### (a) Named mechanisms

1. **Splink** (UK Ministry of Justice) — the reference open-source Fellegi–Sunter implementation: EM-trained m/u, term-frequency-adjusted u-probabilities (a match on "The Blue Note" is worth more than on "Main Street Bar"), match weights in interpretable bits, runs on DuckDB/Spark to 100M+ records. ([Splink docs](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html), [comparison of Splink/Zingg/dedupe](https://tilores.io/content/best-open-source-entity-resolution-and-record-linkage-libraries-splink-zingg-dedupe-and-when-to-move-beyond-them/)). **Zingg** adds Spark-scale active learning (30–50 labeled pairs to train) with a learned blocking model ([Zingg repo](https://github.com/zinggAI/zingg)); **dedupe.io** is the light Python active-learning option for <1M records ([awesome-entity-resolution](https://github.com/OlivierBinette/Awesome-Entity-Resolution)).
2. **Blocking strategies**: token blocking, phonetic keys (Double Metaphone), and increasingly **embedding-ANN blocking** (semantic nearest-neighbor candidate generation catches "Joe's Bar & Grille" ↔ "Joes Bar and Grill" ↔ "the grille on 5th") — the direction of travel per current practice ([entity-resolution-at-scale overview](https://medium.com/@shereshevsky/entity-resolution-at-scale-deduplication-strategies-for-knowledge-graph-construction-7499a60a97c3)). For places, geospatial blocking (H3/geohash cell + neighbors) is the highest-precision cheap blocker.
3. **libpostal** — CRF-based multilingual address parser/normalizer trained on OpenStreetMap/OpenAddresses, ~99% parsing accuracy; `expand_address` produces canonical variants and its **near-dupe hashing** emits blocking keys for address clustering. ([GitHub](https://github.com/openvenues/libpostal), [design writeup](https://www.mapzen.com/blog/inside-libpostal/), [practical matching guide](https://www.crunchydata.com/blog/quick-and-dirty-address-matching-with-libpostal))
4. **Place spines: Placekey and Overture GERS.** Placekey = free universal place ID with a *what@where* structure (address/POI part + H3-derived where part), built for cross-dataset joins ([technical whitepaper](https://www.placekey.io/technical-whitepaper)); **Overture Maps GERS** provides stable open IDs for places/buildings across releases, with Foursquare's open POI corpus folded into Overture Places — a free, commercially-usable venue spine ([GERS docs](https://docs.overturemaps.org/gers/), [Overture Places guide](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/places/), [joining Foursquare OS Places + Overture via Placekey](https://www.placekey.io/blog/joining-foursquare-places-with-overture-places-using-placekey)).

### (b) Transferable design for 1Live

- **Venue spine first, matching second.** Seed `venues` from Overture Places (Foursquare-enriched) filtered to the launch county; store `gers_id`, `placekey`, name, normalized address (libpostal), lat/lng, H3 cell (res 9), name-token set, phonetic keys, and a name embedding. Every extracted venue string resolves *against the spine* rather than pairwise against other candidates — this turns O(n²) dedup into O(n) lookup and gives every event a stable venue ID from day one. National scale = load more Overture tiles; nothing else changes.
- **Resolver = blocked Fellegi–Sunter.** Blocking: union of (same H3 cell ± neighbors) ∪ (shared rare name token) ∪ (embedding ANN top-10). Comparison vector: name Jaro-Winkler banded, address-normalized equality (via libpostal expand), geo-distance banded, phone/website exact. Score with Splink-style weights: e.g., name exact-match m=0.95/u=0.01 → +6.6 bits; geo <100 m m=0.9/u=0.02 → +5.5 bits; accept ≥ threshold bits, queue mid-band for admin review (which you already have as a pipeline stage). Use TF-adjusted u so common venue words ("bar," "cafe," "the") carry ~0 bits. Train m/u by EM on the county's own data once volume allows; hand-set priors until then — both are legible to the evaluator and testable in `tests/test_gates.py`.
- **Partial records are first-class, never dropped.** This is where most event aggregators fail and where the "best systems" pattern applies: represent a candidate event as a **slot-confidence record** — every attribute nullable, each with its own fused confidence (Family 2). A "Friday, venue unknown, artist known" flyer becomes a candidate with `date: σ=0.8, venue: null, artist: σ=0.9`. Completion happens by *re-linkage*: the resolver periodically re-blocks incomplete candidates against new candidates (blocking on artist+date instead of venue+date when venue is null) and **merges claims** when Fellegi–Sunter says same-event. Match keys for events: (venue_id, date) primary; (artist_embedding, date, county) secondary; (title tokens, date) tertiary. An incomplete candidate can never promote (gate requires minimum slot set at minimum σ) but it *earns corroboration weight* the moment its slots are joined — Knowledge Vault's fusion-of-partial-extractions, applied to events.

## Family 4 — Event/signal detection from unstructured streams

### (a) Named mechanisms

1. **GDELT** — the existence proof for planetary-scale event extraction: monitors news in 100+ languages, updating **every 15 minutes**, transforming unstructured text into structured event records (58 fields) coded against the ~300-category CAMEO taxonomy, with parallel streams for events, entities/themes ("GKG"), and imagery. ([gdeltproject.org](https://www.gdeltproject.org/), [data docs](https://www.gdeltproject.org/data.html), [methodology](https://gdeltcloud.com/methodology)) Transferable lessons: fixed micro-batch cadence, a closed taxonomy, per-record source URL provenance, and separating "mention" records from resolved "event" records.
2. **Schema-validated LLM extraction with retry loops.** The production pattern: constrained decoding / structured-output mode → post-inference schema validation (pydantic) → automatic retry with the validation error in context (the Instructor pattern). Provider structured-output modes now hit ~98–100% schema compliance. ([Structured Output in Production](https://tianpan.co/blog/2025-11-10-structured-output-production-llms), [schema validation for real pipelines](https://collinwilkins.com/articles/structured-output), [BentoML handbook](https://bentoml.com/llm/model-interaction/structured-outputs))
3. **Self-consistency for extraction confidence.** Run extraction k times at temperature ~0.8; keep a tuple only if it appears in a strict majority (⌈k/2⌉+1) of runs — agreement rate doubles as an empirical confidence signal. ([SCSG, SemEval-2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.01788), [frugal KG construction with self-consistency](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11104))
4. **Verbalized confidence is NOT calibrated.** Models assign high confidence to wrong answers at nearly the rate of right ones; post-fine-tuning self-reports can sit at a constant 0.95 with ECE 0.174 — self-reported confidence must never be a gate input. Calibration must come from *behavioral* signals (self-consistency agreement, logprobs, downstream corroboration) checked against outcomes. ([practitioner guide, SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/ai/when-the-model-is-confident-and-wrong-a-practitioner-guide-to-llm-output-reliability/), [confidence analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18562))

### (b) Transferable design for 1Live

- **Extraction contract**: pydantic `CandidateEvent` schema (nullable slots per Family 3); provider structured-output mode; on validation failure, one retry with the error message appended; on second failure, dead-letter the fragment with the raw text preserved (never silently drop — that's an escaped-defect vector). This is the exact production-consensus stack and matches the charter's validate-before-gate posture.
- **Confidence = measured, never asked.** `extraction_conf` for a slot = (a) deterministic-parser origin (JSON-LD/iCal) → fixed high prior; (b) LLM origin → self-consistency agreement fraction over k=3 samples for weak-signal text only (cost-gated: only fragments that failed the deterministic waterfall reach the LLM at all, and only genuinely ambiguous ones get k>1); (c) recalibrated monthly against the golden set (a per-extractor reliability curve — Knowledge Vault's extractor-reliability term, and it drops straight into the Family 2 fusion formula).
- **GDELT-shape pipeline**: fixed-cadence micro-batches (your GitHub Actions cron), *mentions* table (every extraction with URL+offset provenance) distinct from *candidates* (resolved events) — auditability per stage is literally GDELT's architecture and already your charter's pipeline invariant. Add GDELT's discipline of a **closed category taxonomy** for event types so extraction is classification-against-enum, not free text.

## Family 5 — Pattern recognition on recurring events

### (a) Named mechanisms

1. **Partial periodic pattern mining** — finding subsequences that recur periodically (with tolerance for misses and shifts: asynchronous/local periodic patterns), with automatic period discovery. ([Mining Asynchronous Periodic Patterns, TKDE](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/TKDE.2003.1198394), [local periodic patterns](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0020025520309567), [multi-sequence periodic mining — Fournier-Viger](https://www.philippe-fournier-viger.com/2019_IS_periodic%20patterns%20multiple%20sequences.pdf))
2. **Wrapper induction / Vertex (Yahoo!)** — learn XPath extraction rules per site template from a few sample pages, then extract from every page sharing the template; production system did 250M+ records from 200+ sites, with explicit machinery for (crucially) **detecting when a site's template changes** by monitoring sample pages. ([Web-scale information extraction with Vertex](https://academia.edu/5381053/Web-scale_information_extraction_with_vertex), [wrapper overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrapper_(data_mining))). Modern successors do zero-shot transfer across sites ([ZeroShotCeres](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07105), [CERES](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.04635)).
3. **Deequ-style metric anomaly detection** (Amazon) — "unit tests for data": declarative constraints plus a **MetricsRepository** of per-run metrics (row counts, null rates, distinctness) with anomaly strategies (e.g., relative-rate-of-change) that fail the run when today's value deviates from history — the canonical answer to "a source suddenly yields 0 or garbage." ([Deequ repo](https://github.com/awslabs/deequ), [SIGMOD 2019 paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3299869.3320210), [anomaly detection example](https://github.com/awslabs/deequ/blob/master/src/main/scala/com/amazon/deequ/examples/anomaly_detection_example.md))

### (b) Transferable design for 1Live

- **Recurrence inference as RRULE hypothesis scoring.** Don't run general periodic-pattern mining — events live on a calendar, so the hypothesis space is tiny and enumerable: for each (venue, normalized-title/series-key) group with ≥3 confirmed instances, score candidate RRULEs — WEEKLY;BYDAY=TU, BIWEEKLY, MONTHLY;BYSETPOS (first-Friday), MONTHLY;BYMONTHDAY — by `support/(support + violations + 1)` (Laplace-smoothed), where a violation is a predicted date with a crawled-and-absent observation (absence only counts when the source was actually fetched that cycle — the asynchronous-pattern tolerance). Store as `series(venue_id, series_key, rrule, confidence, last_confirmed)`. **Uses**: (1) *prediction* — emit a `predicted` candidate for the next instance at prior confidence = series confidence × decay, which the crawler then tries to corroborate (this is a freshness multiplier: you know where to look before the event is posted); predicted-but-uncorroborated instances surface only per founder-ratified display rules, never as confirmed. (2) *Prior* in fusion — a claim matching an established series gets a prior boost; a claim *breaking* a series (Tuesday trivia claimed on Wednesday) gets flagged for the dispute path.
- **Per-source template learning (Vertex, LLM-assisted).** First time a new source is crawled, the LLM extracts *and* emits candidate CSS/XPath selectors for the repeating event block; store as `source_template(source_id, selectors, learned_at, sample_hash)`. Subsequent crawls run the cheap selector-based extractor; the LLM is only re-invoked when (a) template validation fails (selectors return 0 nodes or schema-invalid output) or (b) a sampled page's structure hash drifts — Vertex's site-change detection. This converts per-crawl LLM cost into a one-time cost per source per template-generation, the single biggest cost-per-verified-event lever (§14.2).
- **Source health sentinels (Deequ pattern).** Per source per crawl, persist metrics: candidates yielded, fragment-parse success rate, null-slot rate, schema-validation failure rate, duplicate rate. Nightly anomaly check with a relative-rate-of-change strategy (e.g., fail if yield < 30% of trailing-28-day median, or parse-failure > 3× median); an anomalous source is auto-quarantined — its new claims are held at `unverified` and it stops earning reliability updates until a human or a clean run clears it. This closes the loop with Family 2: a garbage-emitting source must not poison `t(s)` while broken. Wire the check's dead-man ping to healthchecks.io per the Sentinel rule.

## Ranked Top-10: mechanisms by impact-per-build-cost for a truth-first events engine

1. **JSON-LD/iCal/feed-first extraction waterfall** — near-zero build (parsers exist), eliminates LLM cost and extraction error for the large fraction of sources emitting schema.org/Event; highest-confidence claims for free. ([Google Event docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/event?hl=en))
2. **Fellegi–Sunter resolver via Splink over blocked candidates** — mature library, interpretable bits-of-evidence thresholds that map onto your admin-review band; the difference between "dedup by string equality" and top-0.1% linkage. ([Splink](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html))
3. **Overture/Foursquare venue spine + libpostal + Placekey/GERS IDs** — free open data, one-time load per county, converts venue matching from open-ended fuzzy problem into spine lookup and gives global scalability by construction. ([GERS](https://docs.overturemaps.org/gers/), [libpostal](https://github.com/openvenues/libpostal), [Placekey](https://www.placekey.io/technical-whitepaper))
4. **TruthFinder/KBT source-reliability loop feeding an odds-based gate** — a few hundred lines implementing published formulas; this *is* the truth-first moat: source trust earned from agreement history, calibrated per-fact probability, disputes as a mechanical fusion output. ([TruthFinder](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1281192.1281309), [KBT](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol8/p938-dong.pdf), [Knowledge Vault](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Knowledge-vault:-a-web-scale-approach-to-knowledge-Dong-Gabrilovich/cf5ea582bccc7cb21a2ebeb7a0987f79652bde8d))
5. **Cho–Garcia-Molina λ̂ estimator + time-to-event-weighted recrawl** — one formula and one scheduling column; multiplies effective crawl budget (~35% freshness gain from the estimator alone in their experiments) and concentrates checks where cancellations happen. ([Estimating Frequency of Change](http://oak.cs.ucla.edu/cho/papers/cho-freq.pdf), [Kolobov SIGIR 2019](http://erichorvitz.com/SIGIR_2019_crawl_under_politeness_constraints.pdf))
6. **Per-source template learning with LLM-synthesized selectors + drift detection (Vertex pattern)** — moderate build, order-of-magnitude cut in cost-per-verified-event; the mechanism that makes national scale affordable. ([Vertex](https://academia.edu/5381053/Web-scale_information_extraction_with_vertex))
7. **Deequ-style per-source yield anomaly detection with auto-quarantine** — small build (metrics table + threshold job), protects the entire trust system from a single broken source; direct Sentinel/Kaizen fit. ([Deequ, SIGMOD 2019](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3299869.3320210))
8. **RRULE hypothesis scoring for recurrence** — tiny hypothesis space, big payoff: predictive crawling, fusion priors, and a "series-break" dispute detector no event aggregator currently exposes. ([periodic pattern mining](https://www.philippe-fournier-viger.com/2019_IS_periodic%20patterns%20multiple%20sequences.pdf))
9. **Schema-validated LLM extraction with retry + self-consistency-derived confidence (never verbalized confidence)** — mostly configuration atop existing structured-output modes; the calibration discipline is what keeps the gate honest. ([production patterns](https://tianpan.co/blog/2025-11-10-structured-output-production-llms), [calibration warning](https://sdtimes.com/ai/when-the-model-is-confident-and-wrong-a-practitioner-guide-to-llm-output-reliability/))
10. **Slot-confidence partial records with re-linkage completion** — schema decision more than code; lets 1Live hold and later complete the "flyer-grade" events every competitor drops, at the cost of a more careful gate predicate. ([Knowledge Vault fusion](https://lunadong.com/talks/fromDFtoKF_vldb.pdf))
