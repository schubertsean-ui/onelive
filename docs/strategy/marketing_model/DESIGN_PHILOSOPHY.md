# Filled Frame

A movement against the empty grid. The swimlane — three lanes, most cells vacant —
is honest about structure and wasteful about space. Filled Frame inverts the
priority: every square centimeter of the canvas earns its place, and type is the
dominant material. If a region holds no information, the composition contracts
until it does.

**Space and form.** The unit is the card: a titled, bordered rectangle sized to
its content, packed edge-to-edge in a strict grid with narrow, even gutters.
Sequence is carried by numbering and reading order, not by long connector arrows
crossing empty fields. Vertical space is rationed like ink: a card is exactly as
tall as its densest sibling, never taller.

**Color and material.** A near-white paper ground; one ink. Three actor hues —
blue for the owner, orange for the agent, green for the world — appear only as
small chips and card accents, never as fields of decoration. Yellow is reserved
for the single most important gesture: the tap. Color labels; it does not fill.

**Scale and rhythm.** Body type is set large enough to read at arm's length on a
printed landscape page — the test is print, not screen. Hierarchy comes from two
sizes and two weights, no more: a header voice and a body voice. Rhythm comes
from the repetition of identical cards, meticulously aligned, the way a
specimen sheet repeats a form until the variation itself becomes the message.

**Composition and balance.** The page is width-bound; therefore the frame's
aspect is engineered so the printed image bleeds to the margins with no dead
band on any side. Every ledger, chip, and badge is placed to consume residual
space. Nothing overlaps; nothing floats unanchored; margins are breathing room,
not vacancy — the difference is intent, and it is visible.

The finished sheet must look labored over — the product of a craftsman who
counted lines per card, balanced gutters to the millimeter, and rebuilt the
grid until the type could grow no further without touching. Master-level
execution, measured in the absence of waste.
