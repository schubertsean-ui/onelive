## Implications for a longitudinal disclosure-diff + promise-tracking product

- **The academic alpha case is validated but the productization gap is real.** Lazy Prices (2020) plus the 2023–2025 LLM wave (Kim/Muhn/Nikolaev, Lopez-Lira/Tang, Jha et al.) confirm disclosure *changes* and call language carry priced, slowly-incorporated information — yet no incumbent sells language-level promise-fulfillment tracking. Closest neighbors are people-level (Paragon MTR), fraud-level (Hudson Labs), and number-level (Visible Alpha/S&P, Daloopa, AlphaSense guidance), leaving "did management do what it said" as open whitespace.
- **LLM homogenization is an existential threat to naive diffing.** If FY2024+ filings are increasingly LLM-drafted/templated (Foster-Fletcher; Lehner's tone-manipulation result), cosine-similarity diffs degrade and tone scores become gameable. A durable product must diff *claims and commitments* (semantic, entity-resolved promises with deadlines) rather than surface text — and should market this robustness explicitly.
- **Regulatory promise-tracking demand is shifting from mandated disclosure to litigation/consumer-law evidence.** With the SEC climate rule heading to rescission (comment period ends Aug 3 2026), the ESG Task Force gone, CSRD scope cut ~80%, and the Green Claims Directive paused, the US federal mandatory-disclosure tailwind is dead for 3–5 years — but courts, national prosecutors (DWS €25M), ASIC (three penalties), Dutch/German courts, and the EU Empowering Consumers Directive (live Sep 2026) all need exactly what a claims-vs-reality longitudinal engine produces: dated public statements matched against subsequent facts. Position for litigators, NGOs, consumer authorities, and D&O underwriters, not just CSRD compliance.
- **Credibility scores have a measurable market mechanism.** The forecast-credibility literature shows investors underreact to guidance from low-track-record managers (drift), and analysts condition on managers' forecasting records (EAR 2024) — i.e., a systematic guidance-accuracy/promise-keeping history is both an alpha input and a defensible IR/benchmarking product; Paragon's claimed 8%/yr long-short backtest (vendor-sourced, unverified) suggests buy-side willingness to pay for management-quality scores.
- **"Anything-washing" broadens the TAM.** SEC AI-washing actions (Delphia/Global Predictions) show enforcement targeting capability claims generally; a promise-tracker that generalizes beyond green claims (AI claims, capex/jobs commitments, guidance) rides every future washing wave rather than one regulatory cycle.
- **Flagged as unverified:** Mayew et al. SMJ citation details; Hudson Labs "6-months-before-collapse" and Paragon backtest performance (vendor claims); EvasionBench and "First Annual Reports of the LLM Era" details (snippet-only); FTC Green Guides 2026 revision timing (speculative).
