### (b) Implications for a press-release/disclosure aggregation + AI diff-analysis product

- **Government is a validated buyer, but via SupTech budgets, not "media monitoring."** SEC (AI Task Force, 30+ AI use cases incl. filings extraction), ECB Athena, ESMA SupTech programme and GAO-documented regulator AI adoption all prove regulators pay for exactly this capability — position for GSA MAS (SIN 541910-family) and ESMA/NCA SupTech procurement rather than PR-monitoring categories. No specific 2024–26 disclosure-monitoring tender was verified in this pass; a targeted SAM.gov/TED sweep is the next step.
- **EU AI Act cost is real but modest and datable:** as a GPAI *deployer* producing AI summaries, the binding items are Art. 50 (from Aug 2, 2026): machine-readable marking of AI-generated text, user disclosure that content is AI-generated, plus Art. 4 AI-literacy for staff — at €15M/3% penalty exposure. The May 2026 Omnibus delayed *high-risk* duties (to Dec 2027/Aug 2028) but left Art. 50 dates essentially intact; do not plan on a transparency reprieve. Build provenance metadata/watermark tagging into every AI-generated diff/summary now — it doubles as a trust feature.
- **Liability posture:** the SEC punishes *overclaiming* AI (Delphia/Global Predictions/Rimar/Presto), not using it — so describe capabilities with engineering precision in all marketing. Hallucinated summaries carry defamation/misrepresentation risk, but early case law (Walters dismissal) shows prominent disclaimers, human review options, and source-linking (cite the underlying filing for every claim) substantially de-risk; a diff product that always anchors to the primary document is structurally safer than free-form summarization.
- **If sold to broker-dealers/advisers, inherit their compliance surface:** FINRA RN 24-09 + 2025/26 oversight reports mean buyers will demand model-governance documentation, accuracy testing, and audit trails as procurement gates — ship a "compliance pack" (model cards, eval results, provenance logs) as a sales asset. CFA Institute standards similarly push buy-side users to demand explainability and MNPI-free data pipelines.
- **Strong demand tailwind from data instability:** disappearing/altered federal datasets, FOIA degradation, federal ESG-enforcement retreat with state-AG/plaintiff-bar (AI-assisted) enforcement rising — all increase the value of an independent, timestamped archive of what companies and agencies *said*, and diffs showing what changed. "Corporate-promise accountability" demand is migrating from regulators to states, litigants, and journalists — a broader, less procurement-bound customer set.
- **MiFID re-bundling (Directive 2024/2811) lowers friction** for distributing research-adjacent AI content in Europe; no AI-summary-specific MiFID rule exists yet (unverified negative), but ESMA's data-intelligence agenda suggests future disclosure-quality rules would raise, not lower, demand for diff-analysis tooling.

**Flagged unverified items:** (1) any specific SEC/ESMA tender for commercial disclosure-monitoring 2024–26; (2) exact Omnibus grace-period mechanics (Dec 2, 2026 marking grandfather — single-source, snippet-level); (3) absence of any securities-fraud case over hallucinated summaries (negative finding, not proven); (4) GSA SIN-level media-monitoring detail; (5) some 2026-dated sources (Council 7 May 2026 press release, Sidley Jun 2026) were seen only as search snippets, not fetched full-text.
