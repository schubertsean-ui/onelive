## 1. The ripple frame

The pebble drops far from the ticker: a chokepoint closes, a regulator moves, a
sanction lands, unrest starts in a supplier's region — and the effect arrives
at a specific company's fundamentals weeks or months later, through channels
(suppliers, customers, shipping lanes, commodity inputs, regulators) most
screens don't watch. For a long-horizon picker the edge is NOT seeing the
splash first (the fast money owns the splash); it is knowing **which companies
sit in the ripple path and how long the wave takes to arrive**, while the
market is still pricing the epicenter. WorldMonitor-class OSINT provides the
pebble-drops; the edge lives in the **linkage map** (event → exposed entities)
and in the **patience horizon** (documented multi-week drift and propagation
lags that fast capital doesn't hold through).

## 2. What the evidence supports, graded

Grades: **B+** = strong peer-reviewed support (snippet-read); **B** = solidly
documented, persistence debated; **C** = real but weak/conditional for our
horizon; **D** = not usable as a direct signal. All SEARCH-SNIPPET.

| Ripple type | Evidence (key sources) | Grade for multibagger use |
|---|---|---|
| **Supplier/customer network shock propagation** | Barrot & Sauvagnat (QJE 2016): supplier natural-disaster shocks cut customer sales growth ~2pp with significant market-value spillovers, concentrated in hard-to-substitute inputs; US–China Entity-List study (2025): −2.93% CAR for US suppliers of sanctioned firms, +3.48% for substitution beneficiaries; supply-chain "glitch" announcements ≈ −10% firm value (Hendricks-Singhal lineage); 2026 work extends propagation to LLM-embedded textual signals | **B+** — the strongest literature in this space, and it is exactly the "adjacent/indirect risk" the founder named |
| **Post-event drift windows** | PEAD "one of the most solidly documented anomalies" (persistence debated post-2010, defended with text-based surprise measures through COVID); SEC investigation announcements ≈ −6% CAR with continued drift; sanctions event studies: significant negative abnormal returns accumulating over days–weeks (−5.4% CAR/7d Russian sample) | **B** — timing edge exists at days-to-weeks windows after public events; usable for entry/add timing on names already selected fundamentally |
| **Prediction-market probabilities** | Kalshi microstructure study (CEPR 2025): prices informative, improving toward settlement, but favorite–longshot biased; biotech catalyst markets (PDUFA/Phase-3) map onto the same events equities react to; NO validated equity-timing edge demonstrated | **C+** — use as a probability *input* for catalyst calendars, corrected for the documented bias; never as a standalone signal |
| **News sentiment / velocity** | Vendor-documented decay is fast (IR decay ~2–5 days; some persistence to ~3 months, strongest small-caps); academic results mixed; most quantitative decay figures come from the vendor itself | **C** — at our horizon, useful as an *attention gauge* ("has the market noticed the ripple yet?"), not as alpha |
| **Country/political-risk indices** | Hassan et al. PRisk (QJE 2019): firm-level, text-derived, correlates with volatility and firm behavior (retrenchment, lobbying) — snippets do NOT establish directional return prediction; free data at policyuncertainty.com | **C** — a risk-evaluation lens (exposure sizing, thesis stress-testing), not a timing signal |
| **GDELT raw signals** | Data-quality critiques dominate: ~55% key-field accuracy, ~20% redundancy (MDPI 2025), geocoding/coverage bias; prediction results asset- and period-specific | **D** as direct signal — usable only as a coverage/attention *sensor* feeding human attention, never as machine truth |

**Honest bottom line:** the evidence supports OSINT as (a) an **adjacent-risk
lens** with genuinely strong propagation literature behind it, and (b) a
**catalyst/drift timing aid** measured in days-to-weeks — and does NOT support
believing raw OSINT feeds are a validated return predictor. The edge the
founder described is real in the literature primarily through the **linkage
map**, which is precisely the piece WorldMonitor does not have and we planned
anyway (§5).

## 3. Concrete leverage for the multibagger

1. **Adjacent-risk audit (defense, immediately useful):** for every holding /
   watchlist name, maintain an exposure map — revenue and production
   geographies, named suppliers/customers where disclosed, chokepoint and
   commodity dependencies, primary regulators. OSINT pebble-drops (chokepoint
   disruption, sanction, enforcement action, instability shift) then resolve
   mechanically to "which of MY names is in this ripple path?" — the
   non-direct/influential risk evaluation the founder asked for. Evidence
   grade B+ supports taking this seriously; it also sharpens sell/trim
   discipline, which no other signal here does.
2. **Catalyst calendar + drift windows (timing):** earnings dates, regulatory
   decision dates (PDUFA-class), sanction/enforcement events on exposed names —
   with the documented drift windows (days–weeks) treated as the patient
   investor's entry/add windows after adverse-but-survivable events, and
   prediction-market probabilities (bias-corrected) as the ex-ante odds input.
   This is the "event-calendar ingestion" gap the multibagger doc already
   names, fed with OSINT context.
3. **Attention gauge (secondary):** news-velocity on a name/theme as a measure
   of whether the ripple is priced yet — informing patience, not positions.

**Non-negotiable regardless of option — the signal ledger:** WorldMonitor (and
most dashboards) OVERWRITE state; that is the yfinance-ban class. Every signal
we consume is captured into our own **append-only, as-of-known-when signal
ledger** (the promise-ledger storage design reused verbatim: events appended,
never edited; reads reconstruct what was known when). Without this, every
backtest lies (look-ahead/survivorship overstatement of 1–4%+ is documented),
and §6 is impossible.

## 5. The shared backbone (one investment, two ventures)

The linkage map that makes ripples actionable — event → exposed entities — is
an **LEI-keyed entity graph**: who supplies whom, who earns where, who answers
to which regulator. That is the SAME entity graph the Promise Ledger already
plans (claims are LEI-keyed; the market analysis's cross-jurisdiction timeline
whitespace). Build it once: the ledger populates promises per entity; the
ripple engine populates exposure edges; the multibagger consumes both (its
documented role as customer-zero). The retrodiction loop validates both
products with one harness: promise → outcome, and pebble → ripple → price.

## 6. Validation before use (the gate between research and money)

1. Point-in-time signal ledger populated (§4) — no vintage, no test.
2. Per-signal retrodiction against historical episodes (Suez 2021, Shanghai
   2022, Entity-List actions, sanction waves): did the exposure map name the
   right companies? What was the actual lag and magnitude?
3. Multiple-testing discipline: deflated-Sharpe-class corrections; "most
   backtested discoveries are false" is the base rate to design against.
4. Only signals that survive retrodiction may inform sizing/timing — and per
   the crowding literature, expect judgment-heavy signals (ripple mapping) to
   hold value longer than mechanical ones (sentiment scores).
