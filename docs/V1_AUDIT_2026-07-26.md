**Who this is for:** the founder. Plain language, no jargon without an
explanation, every number measured this session rather than quoted from an
earlier document. Where I could not verify something, it says so.

**What I was asked:** *"perform a deep, super deep evaluation of the overall code
built to date. I suspect it's bloated, has unnecessary content, contradictory
content, and is unfocused and has way too many rules to be useful."*

**How I checked:** ran the full gate (`bash tools/validate`), ran the whole test

## The verdict in six sentences

Your suspicion is correct, and it is worse and better than you think. **Better:**
the engineering underneath is genuinely strong — 1,665 passing tests, a real
trust architecture, careful credential handling, deterministic importers, and an
independent non-Claude reviewer that has caught real attacks. **Worse:** the
build has been optimising the *process of building* rather than the *product*, and
the two data pipelines that feed your website are **both broken right now** —
one because of a one-line workflow bug that has meant the local-venue feed has
**never once run on its schedule**, and one because the Anthropic account hit the
**spend cap you set in the console** — which is a setting you can raise in about a
minute, not a date to wait out. *(Corrected 2026-07-26 on your instruction: this
paragraph originally said extraction "will not work again until 2026-08-01", which
presented your own configurable limit as a fact of nature. Nothing in the v1 plan
waits on a date.)*

The single most important structural finding: **you told the agent on 2026-07-25
that you cannot hand-approve thousands of events. The decision was recorded, a
policy module was written, tests were written for it — and it was never connected
to anything.** The charter still says the opposite. So today a long-tail event
physically cannot reach your website without a human click, which is the exact
thing you said doesn't scale.

## 2. What is genuinely good (do not throw this away)

I want to be fair, because a lot of this is real and worth keeping.

1. **The trust architecture is sound and it is actually implemented**, not just
   described: provenance columns, a 4-state confidence model, row-level security
   on the database, a gate that decides promotion separately from the AI that
   extracts, `disputed` always shown. The separation is real in the code, not
   only in the docs.
2. **Credential handling is better than most production systems.** The database
   password is assembled inside a single step, URL-encoded, log-masked, never
   written to `GITHUB_ENV` or step outputs. Untrusted workflow inputs reach the
   shell only through environment variables, never string-interpolated. This is
   correct and I would not change it.
3. **The independent non-Claude reviewer earns its keep.** Reading the review
   history, the second-model seat caught a genuine fail-open bug (a `2>/dev/null`
   that conflated "file missing" with "write failed") that the first model had
   passed twice. That is the mechanism working exactly as intended.
4. **The deterministic importers are the right call.** Ticketmaster / SeatGeek /
   Eventbrite / venue ICS + schema.org feeds need no AI, cost nothing per event,
   and cannot hallucinate. This is your real data foundation and it is built.
5. **The test suite is not decorative.** 1,665 tests, green on a full-history
   clone, and the ones I read assert real behaviour including negative cases.
6. **`/tonight` is a real, working server-rendered feed** reading real rows, with
   an honest failure mode (it explains a misconfiguration rather than showing a
   blank page) and an additive union read so a secondary-source failure cannot
   blank a working feed.

## 5. Where the weight actually is, and what to cut

I looked for what a reader must hold in their head *before writing a line of
code*. Today that is roughly **26,000 words** across `CLAUDE.md`, `STATE.md`,
`TODOS.md` and the five process documents — plus 36 red classes and 46 open
register rows. That is not a contract; it is a syllabus.

Three specific bloat mechanisms, each with a name:

1. **Prose ratchets.** Every review round appends a clause and deletes nothing.
   `CLAUDE.md`'s prime directive 1 is a **single 1,100-word sentence-chain** about
   one exception to one gate — an exception that is already enforced mechanically
   by `tools/classify_extraction_surface.py` and `tools/trust_gate.py`. **The
   prose is not the mechanism.** It can be one sentence plus a link.
2. **Narrative stored as state.** `STATE.md` interleaves 27 session contracts,
   premortem tag lists, and round-by-round review archaeology with the handful of
   facts a new session needs. The Construction Loop's `[S3:…]` tag blocks alone
   run to dozens of lines per contract.
3. **Meta-work that measures meta-work.** Brain IQ scores, reviewer scorecards,
   Kaizen trend meters, held-out brain evals, KPI registries. Each is defensible
   alone. Together they are a second product with no users.

**A correction to this section, made the same day.** The founder's 2026-07-26
directive — *everything is built toward the vision and how it is supposed to make
people feel* — exposed that the first version of this audit had the same blind spot
as the thing it was auditing. It measured bloat in words and rules and never asked
whether the bar described the *product*. It did not: `docs/BAR.md` shipped with 66
rows grading the machine and **zero rows naming the vision, the mission, the promise
to the fan, or the feeling.** That is a bigger defect than any word count on this
page. It is fixed — `docs/BAR.md` now opens with §0 (the vision, quoted from the
ratified brief) and section **P**, fourteen rows on purpose and felt experience,
placed ahead of every engineering section. Full record:
`docs/memory/decisions/2026-07-26_vision-first-directive.md`. And the finding that
fell out of it is sharper than anything in §1: **six of fourteen P rows are met,
while forty-five of sixty-six engineering rows are — the machine is in far better
shape than the experience it exists to deliver, and P1, the ten-second answer, has
never been measured once.**

**A second correction, same honesty.** An earlier draft of this section implied the
simplification cut the rule surface to roughly a third. Measured properly: the
read-before-you-code surface went from **11,770 words across 7 documents to 10,068
across 4** — a **14%** word reduction. The structural gain is real (a bar with
numbers instead of a citation essay, a v1 definition that did not exist, an index
that says what binds); the word cull was modest, and `docs/BAR.md` deliberately grew
to carry the vision. Saying otherwise would have been the exact false-confidence
class this audit exists to name.

**What I changed (this PR):**

- `CLAUDE.md` rewritten from 2,834 words to a short binding contract that now
  **opens with the vision rather than the rules**. Every invariant kept at full
  strength; the exception essay moved to `docs/EXTRACTION_EXCEPTION.md` where the
  mechanism it describes actually lives.
- One process document, `docs/HOW_WE_WORK.md`, replaces the working surface of
  five (`OPERATING_RULES.md`, `KAIZEN.md`, `skills/construction_loop.md`,
  `hats/README.md`, `skills/adversarial_review_v2.md`). The originals stay on disk
  as reference — tools and tests cite them — but they are no longer the thing you
  read to work.
- `docs/BAR.md` is new: **world-class defined once, per aspect, as a number, with
  the gate that enforces it and its honest current status.** This is the "front
  and centre" definition that was requested. `docs/WORLD_CLASS.md` remains the
  citation appendix behind it.
- `docs/INDEX.md` is new: every document classified **CANON** (binding),
  **REFERENCE** (read on demand), or **HISTORICAL** (never a rule). The CANON set
  is small and enumerated there; the surface you must read *before writing code* is
  four files, measured at 10,068 words. *(This bullet said "only 9 documents are
  CANON" until the PR #76 review pointed out that the ratified design brief and
  `docs/memory/RED_CLASSES.md` are also binding — the count was wrong and the
  enumeration in `docs/INDEX.md` is authoritative, not a number restated here.)*
- `LIVE_READINESS.md` deleted and `docs/SPRINT_LIVE_SITE.md` emptied to a
  tombstone pointing at `docs/V1.md` — both were factually false. Git history
  keeps the text. The tombstone rather than a deletion is itself an audit finding
  in miniature: `ingest.yml` cites that path in an error message and sits inside
  the armed cron's runtime closure, so editing one string would have invalidated
  the arming-evidence binding and demanded a fresh **paid** smoke run. The gate
  caught me doing exactly that, and it was right.

**What I deliberately did NOT cut,** because cutting it would lose real quality:
no gate threshold, no test, no check in `tools/validate`, no invariant, and no
mechanical enforcement of any kind. Simplification here means *fewer words
governing the same mechanisms*, never *fewer mechanisms*. Loosening a gate is
your decision, not mine, and I am not asking for one.
